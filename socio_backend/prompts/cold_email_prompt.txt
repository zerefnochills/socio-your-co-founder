## COLD EMAIL

Rules:
- Subject line: Under 8 words. Specific to THEM. No clickbait. No generic "Quick question" or "Collaboration opportunity".
- Opening line: Reference something specific from research — a recent post, a portfolio company, a stated thesis, a company milestone. Never start with "My name is" or "I came across your profile".
- Body: 3-4 sentences max. State what you're building + one concrete traction signal. One sentence on why THEM specifically. Clear single ask.
- Tone: Peer to peer, not supplicant to authority. Write as if you expect a yes.
- No attachments, no decks in first email.
- P.S. line: Optional but powerful — one line human detail that makes you memorable.

## COLD CALL SCRIPT

Structure:
1. Permission opener (5 sec) — "Did I catch you at a bad time?" beats "How are you?"
2. Hook (15 sec) — Who you are + one-line traction signal. Stop. Let them respond.
3. Bridge (20 sec) — Why you called THEM specifically. Make it about their world, not yours.
4. Ask (10 sec) — Single specific ask. Date + duration. "15 minutes next week, Tuesday or Thursday?"
5. Objection responses — prepare 2: "Not interested" and "Send me something"

Keep the script conversational. Write it as spoken words, not sentences. Short. Punchy. Pauses matter.

## FOLLOW-UP SEQUENCE

### Follow-up 1 (Day 3 if no reply)
- Subject: Re: [original subject] — no change
- Add ONE new piece of value: a relevant insight, a press mention, a user quote
- Re-state the ask in one sentence
- Max 4 sentences total

### Follow-up 2 (Day 7 if still no reply)
- New subject line — different angle, same goal
- Acknowledge you've reached out before in one casual line
- Lead with a new angle or framing entirely
- Soften the ask: "Even a quick no works for me"

### Follow-up 3 (Day 14 — the breakup)
- Subject: "Closing the loop"
- 2 sentences only. Acknowledge they may not be interested. Leave the door open with zero pressure.
- This email gets the most replies. Tone = graceful exit, not guilt trip.
